# Contoso Learning: New Hire Module 1, Serving the Customer

Document owner: Learning and Development
Effective date: 2026-01-05
Audience: New support representatives, first week

## Module objectives

By the end of this module a new representative can describe the Contoso service
promise, identify the four stages of a service interaction, and apply the
escalation rules.

## The Contoso service promise

Contoso commits to three things on every interaction: acknowledge the customer's
situation, give an accurate answer or an honest "I will find out", and confirm
what happens next and when.

Speed matters less than accuracy. A fast wrong answer costs more than a slow
correct one, because it generates a second contact and erodes trust.

## The four stages of an interaction

1. **Acknowledge.** Restate the customer's situation in your own words so they
   know they were understood. Do not apologize reflexively; apologize when
   Contoso is at fault.
2. **Establish the facts.** Confirm the order, account, or policy detail before
   answering. Never answer from memory when the record is available.
3. **Resolve or route.** Give the answer, or state plainly that it needs someone
   else and who that is.
4. **Confirm next steps.** End with what will happen, who does it, and by when.

## Language guidance

Use plain language at roughly a sixth grade reading level. Avoid internal
jargon, system names, and department names that mean nothing to a customer.

Prefer the active voice. "We will refund your card" is clearer than "a refund
will be issued".

Never speculate about why something went wrong, and never blame another
department, a carrier, or a system in front of the customer.

## Escalation rules

Escalate immediately, without attempting to resolve, when the customer:

- Alleges a financial loss caused by Contoso
- Mentions legal action, a regulator, or the press
- Describes a safety issue or a medical emergency
- Requests a supervisor for any reason

Escalation is not a failure. Escalating correctly is a measured competency.

## What good looks like

A strong interaction is one where the customer could repeat the answer back
accurately, knows what happens next, and does not need to contact Contoso again
about the same issue.
